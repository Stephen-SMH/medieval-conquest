# Medieval Conquest

A turn-based medieval strategy game in the style of *Strategy & Tactics: Medieval Wars*: every unit is a token on
a map of regions, and you click a unit, then a neighbouring region, to move or attack.

Godot 4.7, GDScript, Compatibility renderer (desktop and web).

The requirements are in [PRD.md](PRD.md); version 2 is section 8.

## Run

Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7 and press F5, or run `godot --path .` from this folder. The game opens on the
menu: campaigns, skirmish, or a saved game.

## Controls

| Action | Input |
|---|---|
| Select a unit | Click its figures |
| Plan a move or attack | With a unit selected, click a **blue** region to move or a **red** one to attack. An attack opens a slider for how many soldiers to send (archers: how many shoot and how many fight hand to hand). Cavalry can then click a second step. |
| Cancel an order | Click the unit again, its arrow, or press U |
| Buy reinforcements | The helmet shield (top left) or R: pick a bundle, confirm, then click a lit province |
| Treasury and research | The coin shield (top left) |
| Menu: save/load, research, battle report, map mode, main menu | The pause shield (top right) or Esc |
| Messages from the last turn | The ! shield (bottom right) |
| End your planning | The hourglass shield (bottom right) or Enter |
| Skip a turn's animation | Click anywhere while it plays |
| Pan / zoom | Drag or WASD / mouse wheel, `=` `-`, or pinch on touch screens |

## How a turn works

Every faction plans at the same time. Each unit gets one order, drawn as a spear-shaped arrow: blue to move, red
to attack (cavalry may take two steps). Nothing happens until everyone has turned the hourglass; then the turn
resolves in a fixed order and plays out on the map: all volleys, then head-on clashes, then moves (units of two
factions moving into the same empty region fight for it), then attacks (several attackers on one region fight
strongest first), then captures and the gold.

- **One unit per region.** Moving into your own region held by the same unit type merges them (up to 20 soldiers);
  two of your units can swap; any other move into an occupied region of yours is blocked.
- **Attacks:** a failed attacker ends the turn where it started. A beaten defender retreats to a free region of its
  own or is destroyed. A detachment that wins holds the region it took; the rest stay home.
- **Ranged units** shoot from their own region (longbows 2 regions away); only enemy archers shoot back, and a volley
  never takes a region.
- **Ranks, generals, healing:** veterans earn gold chevrons; a general (light-blue pennant) adds +10% attack per
  Command; a resting unit heals 2 soldiers a turn (4 with Barracks) for gold.
- A **red X** under a unit means it has no moves left this turn.

## Gold

Gold is the only resource. Every region pays 10 gold a turn (shown on the map), towns 15, the capital 10 more, and
markets 8 more per level (the real-history maps set 5, 10 or 15 per region). Gold pays for reinforcements,
buildings, research and upkeep; if it runs out, soldiers desert. **Reinforcements** come in fixed bundles (e.g. 5
archers for 45 gold, 10 for 75), go to your capital or a province with a town or camp icon, one order per province a
turn, and arrive at the start of your next turn.

## How battles work

A battle has three phases: two ranged volleys, a cavalry charge (infantry meets it, spearmen at full strength),
then up to 6 melee rounds until a side's morale falls to 30 and it routs. Damage follows the counter table in
[data/counters.tres](data/counters.tres) (spearmen ×2.0 against cavalry, cavalry ×2.0 against archers, ...),
terrain, rivers, walls, generals, ranks and morale. The battle report (menu → last battle report) explains the result.

## Look and credits

The map is a stained parchment political map with groups of painted figures for units, banners, spear arrows and
wooden shield buttons. The figures are rendered from CC0 3D models by Quaternius: fetch the models with
[tools/fetch_figure_models.sh](tools/fetch_figure_models.sh) and re-render them with
[tools/render_figures.gd](tools/render_figures.gd) (it needs a window, not `--headless`). Icons are from
game-icons.net (CC BY 3.0); the fonts are Cinzel and IM FELL English (OFL). The UI textures are generated by
[tools/make_theme.gd](tools/make_theme.gd). Full list: [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md).

## Civilizations

| Civilization | Status | Special units | Bonus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Norse | open | Berserker (hits harder as it bleeds, never routs), Huscarl | Raiding: a move can go 2 regions along the coast |
| Anglish | open | Longbowman (shoots 2 regions away), Yeoman | +15% defense in their own regions |
| Frankish | open | Knight (+25% charge), Sergeant | Cavalry 20% cheaper |
| Crusader Orders | open | Brother Knight (best-armoured heavy horse), Mounted Sergeant | Hospitals: resting units heal 2 more soldiers |
| Byzantine | finish 1 campaign | Cataphract, Varangian Guard | +25% gold from markets |
| Saracen | finish 2 campaigns | Mamluk (shoots and rides off untouched), Ghazi | Reinforcements cost 15% less |

21 unit types in all, including Pikemen in the base roster (×2.5 against cavalry, weak to archers and swords; need
Chain Mail). Special units use their base unit's line in the counter table (a Yeoman counts as Spearmen, a Knight as
Heavy cavalry).

## Campaigns and skirmish

Every civilization has a campaign on its own map, 25 missions in all: *The Saga of the Sea-Wolves* (Norse, the
Fjordmark, 5), *The Longbow Wars* (Anglish, the Wealdshire, 5), *Banners of the Valmont* (Frankish, the Valmont, 5),
*The Sunward Vow* (Crusader Orders, the Sunward Coast, 4), *The Purple and the Gold* (Byzantine, the Porphyry Straits,
3) and *Banners of the Sands* (Saracen, the Amber Sands, 3). Objectives include capturing regions, holding a region,
destroying a faction, killing or capturing a named general, surviving with little gold, and conquest. Missions open
in order; the Byzantine and Saracen campaigns open with their civilizations. Your best stars are kept in
`user://progress.json`. `godot --headless -s tools/sim_campaigns.gd` lets the AI play every mission to check the
difficulty. Skirmish lets you pick any map, your
civilization, 1–3 computer opponents and a difficulty; win by conquest.

The maps, region names, campaigns and text are original. The maps are generated by
[tools/make_maps.py](tools/make_maps.py) and the campaigns by [tools/make_campaigns.py](tools/make_campaigns.py).

## Research

Two trees of 6 techs in 3 tiers. One tech at a time, paid in gold when you start it, done after 2–4 turns.

| Economy | Military |
|---|---|
| Crop Rotation: +1 gold from every region | Chain Mail: +10% infantry defense |
| Coinage: +15% gold | Composite Bows: +15% ranged attack |
| Mining: +3 gold from hills, +5 from mountains | Stirrups: +15% cavalry charge; needed for heavy cavalry, knights, cataphracts |
| Logistics: upkeep 20% cheaper | Siege Engineering: recruit catapults; sieges 1 turn shorter |
| Heavy Plough: +2 gold from plains | Drill: new units start as Veterans |
| Guilds: +25% from markets | Tempered Steel: +10% melee attack |

## Hotseat, settings and mobile

- **Hotseat:** 2–4 people share the device, optionally with computer opponents. Each plans in turn behind a "pass
  the device" screen, with their own fog of war; then everyone's orders resolve together.
- **Settings:** turn animations (normal, fast, off), full screen, interface size, and resetting campaign progress.
- **Touch:** taps act as clicks; two fingers pinch to zoom and drag to pan.

## Export

`export_presets.cfg` has two presets:

- **Web** (Thread Support off, so it runs on any static host): `godot --headless --export-release "Web" build/web/index.html`.
- **Android** (arm64, landscape): see below.

### Build for Android

One-time setup on a Mac (the Android export templates must be installed in Godot):

```
brew install openjdk@17
brew install --cask android-commandlinetools
export JAVA_HOME=/opt/homebrew/opt/openjdk@17/libexec/openjdk.jdk/Contents/Home
SDK=~/Library/Android/sdk
yes | sdkmanager --sdk_root=$SDK --licenses
sdkmanager --sdk_root=$SDK "platform-tools" "build-tools;36.1.0" "platforms;android-36" "cmdline-tools;latest"
```

In Godot's Editor Settings (Export > Android), set **Java SDK Path** to the `JAVA_HOME` above, **Android SDK
Path** to `~/Library/Android/sdk`, and **Debug Keystore** to a debug keystore (create one with
`keytool -genkeypair -keyalg RSA -alias androiddebugkey -keypass android -storepass android -keystore debug.keystore -dname "CN=Android Debug,O=Android,C=US" -validity 9999`).

Then build a debug APK (about 30 MB):

```
godot --headless --import    # once
godot --headless --export-debug "Android" build/android/medieval-conquest.apk
```

The launcher icons in `assets/android/` are made from `icon.svg` by `tools/make_android_icons.sh`; run it again
if the icon changes.

To install the APK on a phone (Android 7 or later, 64-bit), do one of the following:

- **USB:** on the phone, open Settings > About phone and tap *Build number* seven times, then turn on
  Developer options > *USB debugging*. Plug the phone in, accept the prompt, and run
  `~/Library/Android/sdk/platform-tools/adb install -r build/android/medieval-conquest.apk`.
- **Copy the file:** put the APK on the phone (cable, cloud drive or email), open it in the Files app, and allow
  *Install unknown apps* for that app when asked.

## Walls and sieges

Walls are built in three levels: palisade, stone walls, castle (a castle icon appears behind the tokens). Defenders
behind walls take 33%, 50% or 60% less damage; each attacking catapult cuts that. Instead of assaulting, you can
**besiege** (a button on a melee unit next to the walls): keep a melee unit next to them and after 2, 4 or 6 turns they are breached, the bonus is gone, and
the starving defenders lose a quarter of their soldiers every turn.

## Missions and the computer players

A mission has objectives (capture regions, hold a region for some turns, destroy a faction), a **par** turn count
and a **hard limit** of twice par. Finishing at or under par gives 3 stars, under 1.5 × par 2 stars, otherwise 1;
passing the limit, losing your capital or losing everything loses the mission. Without a mission, the last faction
standing wins.

Every faction without a human player is run by the AI ([scripts/game/ai.gd](scripts/game/ai.gd)). It recruits units
that counter the enemies it can see, builds when food or gold run short, pulls badly hurt units back, attacks where
its forecast is good enough for its personality (aggressive, balanced or defensive), besieges walls, reinforces
threatened regions and advances on the nearest enemy.
